A Session is always in one of four states. Asleep Sessions cost nothing but disk, so a Runner can hold many more Sessions than it runs at once.

<LifecycleDiagram />

| State        | Sandbox                                         | Agent                 | Prompts                                |
| ------------ | ----------------------------------------------- | --------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| **Active**   | Running                                         | Running               | Delivered in order, one Turn at a time |
| **Asleep**   | Stopped, disks kept                             | Stopped               | The next one wakes it                  |
| **Frozen**   | As the Runner left it                           | As the Runner left it | Wait until the Runner is back          |
| **Archived** | Destroyed; Workspace and the agent's state kept | Stopped               | Refused                                |

## Going to sleep

The Hub puts an Active Session to sleep after 15 minutes ([`HUB_SLEEP_AFTER_MS`](/reference/configuration/#hub)) with none of these:

- a Turn running in the main conversation or a [Thread](/guides/reply-in-a-thread/), a [subagent](/guides/work-with-subagents/) running, or a prompt queued;
- a Participant present;
- a [`kndl forward`](/guides/forward-a-sessions-ports/) connected;
- **Keep awake** on.

**Sleep now** in the Session's header puts it to sleep at once. A Runner also puts its Sessions to sleep before it updates, once their running Turns end.

Asleep, the Sandbox is stopped and its disks are kept. Docker's containers are stopped first, so databases shut down cleanly.

### Put to sleep, saying why

The Hub also puts a Session to sleep, even mid-Turn, when what it runs on goes away. The Session's history says why:

- its Creator removed or replaced their Claude credential;
- its Creator was removed as a Member;
- when its Runner is back after the Hub starts, its Creator has no Models to run it on.

It can't wake until its Creator has something to run it on again. Disconnecting a Provider doesn't put a Session to sleep: its Turns on that Provider's Models fail until the Creator connects it again or switches Model. A removed Member's Sessions stay readable until they're purged.

### Keep awake

**Keep awake** in a Session's header stops it going to sleep when idle. Use it for work that runs with nobody watching, such as a dev server or a long background command. Open ports and background commands don't keep a Session awake on their own ([ADR 0016](/contributing/decisions/0016-forwarding-a-sessions-ports/)).

- Anyone with access to the Repo can turn it on or off. The header, the sidebar and the Runners page show who turned it on.
- Turning it on wakes an Asleep Session.
- It stays on until someone turns it off, presses **Sleep now** or deletes the Session. A restore doesn't turn it back on.
- After a Runner update or restart, the Hub wakes the Session again.

## Waking

Anyone with access to the Repo wakes an Asleep Session with a prompt, **Wake now**, a message to one of its subagents or a Thread, or a `kndl forward` of it. The Runner fetches the Creator's setup token from the Hub, when they have one, and starts the same Sandbox, with its dependencies, caches and Docker data as they were. The agent resumes from its storage, and a run that was going continues as a Turn marked as resumed.

A Session whose Creator has no Models doesn't wake. A replaced token reaches the Session at its next wake.

- **A newer Environment version** makes the Sandbox afresh from its Build at the next wake, on the same Workspace and disks. **Restart** on the **Environment changed** banner does that at once.
- **A Runner behind the Hub** wakes no Session. Prompts wait until the Runner runs the Hub's commit.
- **Subagents** show as Interrupted while the Session sleeps, and go on where they were when the agent starts again.

## When a start fails

If the agent can't start, the Session is Asleep, with the reason in its history. Queued prompts stay queued, and nothing retries on its own: the next prompt or **Wake now** tries again. See [The agent exited](/troubleshooting/sessions/#the-agent-exited).

## Frozen

While its Runner is offline, a Session is Frozen. You can still read it, and prompts wait in its queue. Its Sandbox and Workspace are on that Runner, so it waits for it rather than moving.

The agent runs in the Sandbox, not in the Runner, so it keeps running while the Runner's connection is down or the Runner restarts. When the Runner is back, the Session is Active if the agent is still running, and Asleep otherwise.

## Deleting and restoring

Anyone with access to the Repo can delete a Session, with **Delete Session** in its actions menu. Deleting archives it at once: the Sandbox and its disks are destroyed, and the Workspace and the agent's state are kept. Everyone with access to the Repo can still read it.

- **Undo** on the toast restores it.
- **Archived Sessions** in the sidebar lists each Repo's Archived Sessions, with who deleted each and when. Anyone with access to the Repo restores one with **Restore**.

A restored Session is Asleep, and the next prompt wakes it in a new Sandbox. The conversation carries on. Dependencies and Docker's data went with the old disks, so the Start script or the agent reinstalls them.

## Purging

30 days after it was archived ([`HUB_ARCHIVE_RETENTION_MS`](/reference/configuration/#hub)), the Hub purges a Session: its history, its queue, and on its Runner, its Workspace and disks. Work the Session never pushed is gone with it.

To free a Runner's disk sooner, anyone with access to the Repo can purge an Archived Session by hand, not just its Creator ([ADR 0027](/contributing/decisions/0027-anyone-who-sees-an-archived-session-can-purge-it/)). In **Archived Sessions**, press **Purge** on one, or tick several and press **Purge selected**, then confirm. A purged Session can't be restored. If its Runner is offline, the Session is gone from the Hub at once, and its Runner frees the disk when it reconnects.

## History

Every start, sleep, wake, delete, restore and failure is in the Session's history, with who did it.
